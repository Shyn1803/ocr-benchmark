Clark’s question was ﬁnally answered in 1995 by Murakami and Yasuhara [30] using the GordonLitherland pairing. To do this, they ﬁrst proved the following necessary condition for a knot K to bound a β1 = 2 nonorientable spanning surface F.

- Theorem 4.1 (Murakami-Yasuhara [30]). There is a pair of curves generating H1(F;Z) such that the Goeritz matrix of F takes the form:

GF(K)= 

l m m n

and satisﬁes σ(K)= sign(GF(K))−(l + 2m + n), where l,n are odd and m is even.

Consider, as in [30], the knot 74. This knot has 3-genus 1 and hence C(74) ≤ 3. Since 74 is hyperbolic, we have C(74) ≥ 2. It therefore suﬃces to show that C(K) ≠ 2. Now, suppose that C(K) = 2. Since 74 has determinant 15, one can write out integral binary quadratic forms with determinant 15 (see e.g. Conway-Sloane [8]). Using a case analysis, it is then possible to show that no such binary quadratic form satisﬁes Theorem 4.1. Hence, it follows that C(74)= 3 = 2⋅g(74)+1.

Crosscap numbers are in general diﬃcult to compute. Yet progress is currently being made. For example, Kalfagianni-Lee [19] determined upper and lower bounds on the crosscap numbers for several families of knots in terms of the coeﬃcients of the Jones polynomial.

4.3. Non-orientable slice genus. The (smooth) 4-genus of a knot K is the smallest genus among all surfaces properly embedded in B4 such that ∂F = K. A well-known lower bound on the 4-genus is σ(K)  2. In analogy with crosscap number, the (smooth) non-orientable slice genus γ∗(K) of a knot K in S3 is deﬁned as the smallest ﬁrst Betti number of all compact connected non-orientable surfaces F smoothly embedded in B4 such that ∂F = K. If K is slice, then γ∗(K) is deﬁned to be 0. In 1975, Viro [39] showed that γ∗(41)= 2, and hence 41 does not bound a Möbius band in B4.

Another proof that γ∗(41) = 2 follows from a result of Yasuhara, which is proved using the Gordon-Litherland pairing. Recall that the quadratic form q ∶ H1(F;Z 2Z) → Z 2Z of a knot K is deﬁned by q(x) = x⊺Ax, where F is a Seifert surface of K and A is a Seifert matrix for F. The Arf invariant of K is the Arf invariant of this quadratic form. Hence, Arf(K) = 0 if q maps a majority of elements of H1(F;Z 2Z) to 0 and otherwise Arf(K) = 1. The Arf invariant is also the (mod 2) coeﬃcient of z2 in the Conway polynomial of K. Note that there is a well-deﬁned injection Z 2Z → Z 8Z given by x → 4 ⋅ x.
