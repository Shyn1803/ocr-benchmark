However, cases of acute stent malapposition with a large volume or acute stent malapposition located within the edges of the stent were not resolved in most cases. Thus, acute stent malapposition volume and location within stent edges were identified as independent predictors of late-persistent stent malapposition. Our results indicate that an acute stent malapposition with a larger volume or area is more likely to persist at follow-up examinations, which is consistent with findings from previous studies.4,6,8 Late-persistent stent malapposition could be differentiated from resolved acute stent malapposition by an acute

stent malapposition area >1.2 mm2 according to an IVUS study.6 A strut-to-vessel distance ≤260 µm on poststent OCT images was suggested as a cut-off value for resolved acute stent malapposition.18 A volume of acute stent malapposition >2.56 mm3 best separated late-persistent stent malapposition lesions from resolved acute stent malapposition lesions in the present study.
