However, the combinatorial deﬁnition of κ-meagre sets coincides with the topological notion if and only if κ is countable or inaccessible, as was shown in [BHZ, §4]. Even though the ideals are distinct if κ is uncountable but not inaccessible, it is an open question whether the cardinal invariants given by the combinatorial κ-meagre ideal are consistently distinct from those given by the topological κ-meagre ideal:

Question 2.3. Let the combinatorially κ-meagre ideal Mcombκ be deﬁned as in [BHZ, Def. 4.4]. Is non(Mcombκ ) < non(Mκ) or cov(Mκ) < cov(Mcombκ ) consistent for κ that are not inaccessible?

The characterisation of cof(N) and add(N) from Theorem 2.1 cannot be generalised to the higher context, not just because we would need a higher analogue of N, but also because the cardinality of dhκ(∈∗) is highly dependent on the choice of h ∈ κκ when κ is inaccessible, as we will discuss in Section 3.2. For successor κ, Theorem 2.1 looks very diﬀerent:

Theorem 2.4 ([Hyt06], [MS, Thm. 4.6]). If κ is a regular successor, then bκ(✟=✟∞) = bhκ(∈∗) = bκ and dκ = dhκ(∈∗) ≥ dκ(✟=✟∞). If we also assume κ<κ = κ, then the last inequality becomes an equality.

Question 2.5 ([MS, §4]). Is dκ(✟=✟∞) < dκ consistent for κ a successor cardinal and κ<κ > κ?

It was observed by Truss and Miller that the cardinal characteristics add(M) and cof(M) can be expressed in terms of the other cardinals of the Cichon´ diagram.

- Theorem 2.6 ([Tru77], [Mil81]). add(M) = min{b,cov(M)} and cof(M) = max{d,non(M)}.

The same characterisation generalises to the higher Cicho´n diagram if we make strong enough assumptions on κ. We note that this is not provable for all uncountable κ. For example, Brendle [Bre22] showed that κ<κ < cof(Mκ), and thus max {dκ,non(Mκ)} < cof(Mκ) is consistent, and holds whenever max{dκ,non(Mκ)} ≤ κ<κ.

- Theorem 2.7 ([Bre22]). add(Mκ) = min{bκ,cov(Mκ)} and cof(Mκ) ≥ max {dκ,non(Mκ)}. Under assumption of κ<κ = κ the inequality becomes an equality.


As for the other eight cardinal characteristics of the classical Cichon´ diagram, they appear to be as independent as is possible in the following sense: any assignment of the cardinalities ℵ1 and ℵ2 to the cardinals of the classical Cichon´ diagram compatible with both Theorem 2.6 and the arrows in the diagram, is consistent (see [BJ95, Sections 7.5 and 7.6] for each of these cases). This implies that the classical Cichon´ diagram is complete, as no arrows are missing from the diagram. In fact, Goldstern, Kellner, Mejı´a, and Shelah [GKMS21; GKS19] showed that all eight cardinal characteristics can have mutually diﬀerent cardinalities within the same model.
