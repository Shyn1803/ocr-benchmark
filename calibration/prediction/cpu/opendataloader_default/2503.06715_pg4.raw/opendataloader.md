3. Morita Equivalence of Subrings for Rings with Local units

When we refer to Morita equivalence in this paper, we are referring to the notion of Morita equivalence in rings with local units ﬁrst developed by Abrams in [4] and further explored by Anh and Marki in [24]. We will denote Morita equivalent rings with local units R and S as R ∼M S.

In this section, we present a technique to prove that a subring S ⊆ R of a ring with local units is Morita equivalent. We will later apply this to the partial skew group rings and derive various conditions to make the rings Morita equivalent. Most of this section is a generalization of the ideas presented in [3] and [10] to prove a desingularization result for graph and ultragraph algebras respectively.

A signiﬁcant generalization of this section is removing the countability assumption used previously by being slightly more careful with our directed limits. This lets our results apply to all rings with local units instead of just rings with σ-units, which will be useful later when we apply our results to objects that aren’t necessarily countable.

- Deﬁnition 3.1 ([24, Deﬁnition 1]). A ring R (not necessarily unital) is called a ring with local units if there exists some set E ⊆ R such that elements of E are idempotents,


commute with each other, and e∈E eRe = R.

For a ring R with local units, the category of (left) R-modules is deﬁned to be the modules M, with the additional requirement RM = M. These are often called unitary modules. Morphisms of R-modules are deﬁned in the usual way.

Rings with local units R and S are said to be Morita equivalent if their respective categories R-Mod and S-Mod of (unitary) modules are equivalent.
