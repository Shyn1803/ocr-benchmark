The challenge is to provide an intrinsic geometric interpretation of the family of graded linear series W t • . When X = C 1 × ... × C n , intersection theory on Bl x X implies that W t • is contained in a family of complete linear series associated to divisors on X Σ , the blow-up of P n − 1 in n points in general linear position. We show that this inclusion is asymptotically an equality. For this we use that X Σ is a toric variety and the asymptotic behaviour of complete linear series on them can be managed through convex geometry. As a consequence, the vertical slices of ∆ x ( L ) are NObodies associated to a ray of divisors on X Σ with respect to a generic (strict transform) linear ﬂag. Lastly, the global (gluing all t   0) NObody on this special ray of divisors on X Σ is exactly the convex set described in the statement, as we are able to prove the existence of sections whose valuation are the vertices of the simplex.

For box-products of arbitrary degrees the vertical slices of ∆ x ( L ) are still a family of NObodies on X Σ with respect to general linear ﬂags. But it is harder to guess the vertices. It seems an interesting problem to ﬁnd an algorithm that starts with a polytope in R n − 1 that is a NObody on a toric variety with respect to a T -invariant ﬂag, and construct another polytope, that will be a NObody on the same toric space but with respect to a generic linear ﬂag.

In dimension n   3, we can ﬁnd non-trivial sections on X Σ , whose valuations provide the vertices of a slice of the global NObody on X Σ with respect to general linear ﬂags. More precisely:

Theorem 1.2. Let X = C 1 × ... × C n be a product of smooth complex projective curves, polarized by a box product L = L 1 ⊠ ... ⊠ L n with deg L i = d i . Assume d 1   d 2   ...   d n > 0 . Let x ∈ X be any point.

In both cases, the shapes degenerate to the simplex in Theorem 1.1 when d i = 1 for all i, while when the d i are not all equal the shapes are not simplicial.

It turns out that the verticies of our iNObodies from Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 happen exactly when there is a change in the associated base loci of the graded linear series W t • . These ideas are generalized by the authors in [ FL25b ] on any projetcive variety X by connecting three aspects of local positivity of a line bundle L on X at a point x ∈ X . On one hand we have inﬁnitesimal successive minima, then jets and ﬁnally polytopal upper and lower bounds on the generic iNObodies. Moreover, in [ FL25c ] the authors study the shape of generic iNObodies on Jacobians in small dimensions. The main issue here is realizing at least asymptotically the family W t • as complete graded linear series on a modiﬁcation of P n − 1 . For example, for non-hyperelliptic Jacobian threefolds, W t • becomes complete on a birational model of P 2 that is far from being toric, while for the hyperelliptic case we may need to go to the Zariski–Riemann space.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we review the Newton–Okounkov body construction and their basic properties. In Section 3 we use intersection theory, positivity, toric geometry and Euclidean volume computations to understand the vertical slices as Newton–Okounkov bodies on X Σ . In Section 4 we treat the case of dimension n   3. In Section 5 we use particular incomplete linear systems on X Σ to prove that the vertices of the simplex from Theorem 1.1 do belong to the iNObody, concluding its proof. In Section 6 we state and motivate some open questions.
