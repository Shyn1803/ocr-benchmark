# What are transition metals?

The elements in the periodic table are often divided into four categories: main group elements, transition metals, lanthanides, and actinides. The main group elements include the active metals in the two columns on the extreme left of the table and the metals, semimetals (metalloids), and nonmetals in the six columns on the far right.

The transition metals, or transition elements, are the metallic elements in the columns in the center of the table, forming a bridge or transition between the active metals on the left and the elements on the right.

Lanthanides and actinides are usually shown in two rows below the main table; they are sometimes called the inner transition metals because their atomic numbers fall between the first and second elements in the last two rows of the transition metals (after lanthanum and actinium, respectively).
